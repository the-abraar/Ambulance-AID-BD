A pilot proposal for Bangladesh

Ambulance-AID-BD

Faster dispatch and vehicle visibility for ambulance fleets.

প্রতিটি মিনিট মূল্যবান

Working prototype. No operator yet. That is the reason for this note.

The problem

The clock that matters starts at the call, not at the hospital.

  • Minutes are lost between a call and a vehicle on scene, and nobody can see where.
  • Dispatchers work by phone and radio, without live vehicle positions.
  • There is no reliable record of who sent what, when, so nothing can be improved or held to account.

Why now

The pieces are finally in place.

A national call system exists999 already brings emergencies in. What is thin is the dispatch-to-scene step, and the data on it.
Phones are already in drivers' handsA low-end Android phone and a browser should be enough (UNVERIFIED: not yet run on a real phone).
Bangla firstThe driver app works in Bangla and English, so the person on the road is not left out.
Dhaka will not get less crowdedEvery minute of delay needs to be seen to be reduced.
What exists today built

A working dispatch prototype.

Dispatcher consoleCreate, assign and cancel missions; mission history. Vehicles on a map (OpenStreetMap), free, busy or stale, refreshed every 5 s, with an alarm when a driver has not accepted a mission in 2 minutes. Tested in headless browsers and an Android emulator, not yet on real phones.
Driver app (PWA)Bangla and English. Low-end phones: UNVERIFIED, never run on a real phone. Offline queue: GPS pings up to 5 minutes old are sent when signal returns; older ones are dropped.
Status-change audit trailEvery status change records who, from which state, to which, and when, including a dispatcher's change on a driver's behalf, with its reason.
Role-based accessDispatchers manage. A driver can only touch the mission assigned to them.
Token controlPer-user hashed tokens. Rotate, revoke, and automatic 90-day expiry.
Runs on PostgreSQLAutomatic schema migrations and API contract tests. Not yet deployed.
How a mission runs

One clear path from dispatch to hospital, every step on record.

Dispatched
En route to pickup
At scene
Transporting patient
At hospital
Completed

A mission can be cancelled before transport. Each move is logged, so the time between steps can be measured for the first time.

Step names and cancel rules are the developer's first choice and will be confirmed with a real dispatcher before any pilot.

What it can do for Bangladesh

Goals the pilot will measure, not promises.

Response timeGoal: shorten dispatch to acknowledge. Today there is no baseline; the pilot produces one.
AccountabilityA timestamped status-change trail for every mission. This already works in tests.
Data for planningWhere fleets wait, which routes are slow, how many missions are late.
Local software, local jobsBuilt in Bangladesh, open source (Apache 2.0), no licence fee to a foreign vendor.
Low costMeant to run on phones operators already own (UNVERIFIED). Pricing is not set; goal is to stay far below imported systems.
A base for moreOne day, links to 999, only with a written agreement.
Safety and privacy

Advisory only. Phone and radio always come first.

  • If the system is down, operators go straight back to phone and radio. A runbook is drafted.
  • Drivers are told in writing, in Bangla, what is tracked, and consent is recorded.
  • No patient names, vitals or caller numbers are collected in the pilot. Test data only until counsel has reviewed data-protection law.
  • No traffic-signal control exists. It would be considered only after written safety gates: authority agreement, engineer-signed safety case, security review.
The pilot

One fleet. Under 20 vehicles. Dispatch and visibility only.

Proposed, to agree with the operator8 weeks, compared with the operator's own records.
Missions recorded: at least 90% of those actually run.
Complete status trail: at least 80% of missions.
Vehicle pings on at least 90% of missions.
Also measuredMedian dispatch-to-acknowledge time, reported as measured.
Dispatchers: faster or equal to phone and radio?
Serious incidents caused by the tool: zero.
Would the operator continue?

Failure is a valid result and will be published.

Honest status

A working prototype with no operator. That is the ask.

True todayHeadless-browser tests with a fake GPS fix. Backend tests pass on PostgreSQL in GitHub CI. Code is open source.
Not yet trueNo real vehicles, phones in motion, users, partner, legal opinion, or deployment. No measured results.

Software alone cannot close this gap. One operator and one introduction can.

The ask

Three things that are easy to grant.

  • One introduction to an ambulance fleet operator willing to host a small pilot.
  • One named point of contact who can say yes or no and answer questions.
  • Guidance on data-protection compliance for location data of drivers and vehicles.

Nothing here needs a budget, a procurement, or a policy change.
